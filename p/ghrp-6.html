    GHRP-6

    Growth hormone releasing peptide-6

    Unapproved Growth hormone secretagogue Anti-doping: S2.2

    What it is

    A synthetic hexapeptide from the 1980s, one of the first compounds found to trigger growth hormone release.

    How it works

    Activates the ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a) in the pituitary and hypothalamus, causing growth hormone release and strong hunger.

    Use

    No approved uses. Used in research.

    What the research shows

    Animal studies and small human studies, no phase 3 trials. On the WADA prohibited list (S2).

    In depth

    GHRP-6 is a synthetic hexapeptide developed in the 1980s by Cyril Bowers's team. It was one of the first substances known to release growth hormone, even before it was discovered that such substances act through the ghrelin receptor (1999).

    It has no approved human use. It clearly increases hunger and can raise cortisol. Its history led to newer drugs such as anamorelin. WADA prohibits it in sport.

    Advantages

    • Used in research for growth hormone release
    • Small human studies exist

    Disadvantages

    • Strongly raises appetite and can raise cortisol and prolactin
    • On the WADA list (S2) and with no phase 3 trials

    Common effects

    • Strong hunger within minutes of use
    • Flushing and warmth
    • Water retention
    • Drowsiness

    What we do not know

    It raises the stress hormone cortisol and the hormone prolactin. It is not an approved medicine, and there are no long-term safety data.

    What to watch for

    Strongly increases appetite and may raise cortisol and prolactin. Affects the growth hormone axis. There are no studied combinations with medicines or other peptides. Combining them is a doctor's decision.

    Anti-doping

    Listed by WADA in class S2.2, prohibited at all times. WADA
