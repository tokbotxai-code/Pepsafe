    Melanotan II

    Unapproved Alpha-MSH analogue Anti-doping: S0

    Group

    Melanocortin receptor agonists

    Do not combine: duplicated receptors

    Group

    What it is

    An unapproved peptide sold for tanning.

    How it works

    Activates several melanocortin receptors, not only MC1R.

    Use

    No approved uses.

    What the research shows

    Not approved. Mole changes were reported. An FDA committee referred it for further consultation.

    In depth

    Melanotan II is a cyclic alpha-MSH analogue developed in the 1980s at the University of Arizona. Unlike afamelanotide it acts on several melanocortin receptors at once, so besides tanning it causes nausea, reduced appetite and spontaneous erections.

    It is not approved anywhere in the world but is sold online and in salons 'for tanning'. The MHRA and other agencies have warned against it; rapid mole changes and new moles have been reported, as have melanoma cases in users. A melanotan tan does not protect against skin cancer.

    Advantages

    • Drives skin darkening in studies
    • The FDA committee referred it for further consultation

    Disadvantages

    • Changes in moles have been reported
    • Can raise blood pressure and is not approved

    What to watch for

    Risk of skin changes and high blood pressure. Mole changes need a dermatologist. Human safety is not established.

    Anti-doping

    Listed by WADA in class S0, prohibited at all times. WADA
