    Retatrutide

    Reta, LY3437943

    In clinical trials Triple GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon agonist Anti-doping: S0

    Group

    GLP-1 agonists and related (GIP, glucagon)

    Do not combine: duplicated mechanism

    Group

    What it is

    An experimental Eli Lilly peptide given once weekly. It adds the glucagon receptor to tirzepatide's action.

    How it works

    Activates three receptors: GIP and GLP-1 (insulin, appetite) and glucagon (energy expenditure and liver metabolism).

    Use

    Studied in: obesity, type 2 diabetes, knee osteoarthritis pain, sleep apnea, fatty liver disease.

    What the research shows

    Phase 3 results announced by the maker in 2025–2026 (weight and A1C reduction). Not approved; the maker plans to file in 2027.

    In depth

    Retatrutide (LY3437943) is an experimental Eli Lilly peptide that stimulates three receptors at once: GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon, which is why it is called a 'triple agonist'. The glucagon receptor is meant to raise energy use and fat burning in the liver, while GIP and GLP-1 reduce appetite and improve insulin release.

    In the phase 2 trial (NEJM 2023), participants with obesity on the highest dose tested lost on average 24.2% of body weight over 48 weeks, versus about 2% on placebo. A separate analysis also showed less liver fat. The maker announced phase 3 results (the TRIUMPH trials) in 2025–2026, but the drug is not approved anywhere yet; a filing is planned for 2027.

    In trials the most common effects were nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting and constipation, plus a dose-related rise in heart rate. Products sold online as 'retatrutide' or 'reta' do not come from the maker, and nobody checks their content or sterility. Safety outside clinical trials is not established.

    Advantages

    • Largest weight loss seen in trials so far: 24.2% in 48 weeks in phase 2
    • Acts on three receptors, including glucagon, and lowers liver fat

    Disadvantages

    • Not approved anywhere, with a filing planned for 2027
    • Products sold online do not come from the maker and are unchecked
    • Nausea, diarrhoea and a dose-related rise in heart rate

    Common effects

    • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea and constipation, mostly mild to moderate and mainly during dose increases
    • A faster resting heart rate that rose with the dose in the phase 2 study
    • Reduced appetite

    Serious: get medical help

    • Dehydration from vomiting or diarrhoea
    • Stomach pain that does not go away

    What we do not know

    It is not approved. Results come from trials in a few thousand people, so rare effects, long-term safety and effects on muscle and the heart are not yet known. Products sold online under this name do not come from the maker, so their contents and effects are unknown.

    What to watch for

    Do not combine with tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), semaglutide or other GLP-1 agonists: double action on the same receptors, no data. Products sold as retatrutide outside trials do not come from the drug's maker. Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting and constipation were common in trials.

    Anti-doping

    Listed by WADA in class S0, prohibited at all times. WADA
