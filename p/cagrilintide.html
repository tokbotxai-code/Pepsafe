    Cagrilintide

    In clinical trials Long-acting amylin analogue

    Group

    Amylin analogues

    Do not combine: duplicated mechanism

    Group

    What it is

    A Novo Nordisk peptide studied mainly together with semaglutide (CagriSema).

    How it works

    Mimics amylin, a satiety hormone released together with insulin.

    Use

    Studied in: obesity.

    What the research shows

    Phase 3; an application for CagriSema has been filed.

    In depth

    Cagrilintide from Novo Nordisk is a long-acting analogue of amylin, a satiety hormone released by the pancreas together with insulin. An attached fatty acid allows once-weekly dosing. Amylin acts in the brain differently from GLP-1, which is why it is paired with semaglutide.

    In the phase 3 REDEFINE 1 trial (NEJM 2025), cagrilintide plus semaglutide (CagriSema) led to about 20% average weight loss over 68 weeks, more than either component alone; cagrilintide alone gave about 12%. It is not approved yet. 'Cagri' products sold online are not the maker's medicine.

    Advantages

    • Long-acting amylin analogue in phase 3
    • Studied alongside semaglutide in controlled ratios

    Disadvantages

    • Not approved yet
    • Mixing it yourself with other products is untested

    What to watch for

    The combination with semaglutide is studied in fixed proportions. Mixing it yourself with other products is not the same and there are no data.
