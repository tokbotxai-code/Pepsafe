    Ixazomib

    Ninlaro

    Approved drug Proteasome inhibitor

    What it is

    The first proteasome inhibitor in capsule form, which allows treatment at home rather than in hospital.

    How it works

    Reversibly blocks the proteasome like bortezomib, but the molecule is absorbed from the gut.

    Use

    Relapsed multiple myeloma, combined with lenalidomide and dexamethasone.

    What the research shows

    FDA approved in 2015 on the basis of the phase 3 TOURMALINE-MM1 trial.

    In depth

    Ixazomib is the first proteasome inhibitor in capsule form, which allows myeloma to be treated at home rather than in hospital. The FDA approved it in 2015 (Ninlaro) together with lenalidomide and dexamethasone.

    In the TOURMALINE-MM1 trial (NEJM 2016), adding ixazomib lengthened the time until the disease progressed compared with placebo. The most common side effects are diarrhoea, rash, low platelets and neuropathy. Despite the convenient form it is a cancer drug taken on a strict schedule.

    Advantages

    • Approved by the FDA in 2015 for relapsed myeloma
    • Taken by mouth, with the first oral proteasome inhibitor

    Disadvantages

    • Diarrhoea, rash and a fall in platelets
    • Specialist cancer treatment only

    Common effects

    • Diarrhoea or constipation
    • Nausea and vomiting
    • Swelling of the legs
    • Back pain
    • Rash

    Serious: get medical help

    • Low platelet count: bleeding and bruising
    • Nerve damage
    • Severe skin reactions

    What we do not know

    It is an oral chemotherapy for multiple myeloma used under cancer-centre supervision.

    What to watch for

    Diarrhoea, rash, falling platelets. Despite the convenient form it is a cancer drug, taken on a strict schedule set by a haematologist.
