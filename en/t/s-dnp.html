    DNP

    2,4-dinitrophenol, an industrial chemical that has killed people

    Other drugs sold alongside steroidsOral

    Chemical structure (PubChem)

    This section is information, not advice. We give no doses, no routes to buy and no instructions for use. Most of these drugs are controlled or prescription-only, and the safe way to any hormone treatment is a doctor.

    What it is

    DNP, or 2,4-dinitrophenol, is an industrial chemical used in dyes and explosives. It is not a medicine, and in the 1930s it was sold as a weight-loss pill until it was found to be too dangerous.

    How it works

    It makes cells waste the energy from food as heat instead of storing it. That can push body temperature up without any way to cool down.

    Medical use

    It has no medical use. The US declared it unsafe for human use in 1938.

    Risks and side effects

    DNP can cause fatal overheating, fast heart rate, sweating, cataracts and organ failure, and there is no antidote. Deaths have been reported in the UK and the US, including in young people, and the gap between a harmful and a deadly amount is small.

    What not to combine

    No combination is safe. Heat, hot weather, exercise, stimulants and alcohol all increase the risk of fatal overheating.

    Legal status

    Not approved for human consumption anywhere. In the UK the Food Standards Agency treats it as unfit for people, and selling it for use as a diet pill is illegal.

    Sport and anti-doping

    This is not mainly a doping issue. The danger is to life itself.

    Advantages

    • None that outweigh the risk
    • Useful only as a historical warning
    • Studied for its biochemistry

    Disadvantages

    • Can kill, with no antidote
    • Tiny margin between harm and a fatal amount
    • Banned for human consumption
